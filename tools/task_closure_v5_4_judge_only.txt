### 3. TASK CLOSURE DEFINITION & BOUNDARIES
Raw label Y = defect detected = FAIL. Raw label N = no defect = PASS.

A request is CLOSED when it has reached a terminal outcome within the agent's responsibility:
- the requested answer, diagnosis, or feasible resolution;
- a required terminal action, completed AND accurately communicated;
- a justified final refusal or limitation, explained with required next steps;
- an actual transfer of responsibility, communicated to the customer;
- an appropriate completed alternative that the customer accepts.

Boundaries (apply all):
1. NOT PROCEDURE FOLLOWING: An omitted intermediate SOP step, button, optional field, or script is not Y if the goal still reached an appropriate terminal outcome. An unperformed TERMINAL action is different.

2. EXECUTION VS COMMUNICATION: Treat executing a terminal action and informing the customer of its result as separate responsibilities.
   - A verified backend action establishes execution only. An internal ACTION log alone does not show the customer was told, and a promise made before execution does not prove completion.
   - When the outcome must be communicated, the dialogue must convey that the action was completed. Clear completion wording tied to the action counts; silence, a bare "Okay", a topic change, or vague reassurance does not. A truthful customer-visible system confirmation also counts.
   - Judge the whole dialogue. Do not demand exact wording, repetition, or an extra post-action message if the result was already communicated. Customer gratitude never proves closure.

3. SATISFACTION IS NOT EVIDENCE: Gratitude or "that's all" cannot cure a missing terminal obligation; dissatisfaction does not undo a completed one.

4. SOP OBLIGATIONS VS CUSTOMER GOALS: The SOP defines what the agent must do to fulfill the customer's actual chosen request; it does not create new customer goals.
   - Do not turn every SOP instruction, supporting step, optional detail, or script line into a separate obligation.
   - A missing SOP step is a Task Closure failure only when it blocks a necessary terminal outcome (the state change, removal, refund, shipment, or answer that actually fixes the customer's problem).
   - Do not require information to be repeated when the customer already knows or supplied it, unless confirming it is necessary to fulfill the request.
   - Separate procedure violations (procedure_following) from unfinished customer responsibilities (task_closure).

5. NOT HANDOFF QUALITY: A completed, communicated transfer closes the agent's task even if the other team hasn't solved it (quality belongs to good_handoff). A promise to escalate is not a transfer.

6. MISSING INPUT / DROPOFF: If a genuine customer prerequisite blocks completion and the agent explained the next step or limitation, do not manufacture fault. If the agent could still complete a necessary terminal step, abandoning it may be Y.

7. CHOSEN RESOLUTION REPLACES ABANDONED PATHS: Once the customer validly chooses one path (e.g., cancellation over a fee waiver), the abandoned path is not a separate outcome; the chosen path must still be completed.

8. OUTCOME-TO-REQUEST MATCHING: A completed backend action closes a request only if it fulfills the customer's actual selected terminal goal.
   - Check that the action addresses the requested item, account issue, refund, or resolution; an executed refund, update, or purchase is not automatically sufficient.
   - Keep this distinct from action_accuracy: a wrong amount or parameter alone is not a Task Closure failure unless the evidence shows the selected goal stayed unfulfilled.
   - Never infer wrong targeting or action failure from missing or ambiguous parameters, and never re-describe a successful ACTION as a failure. A completion claim that trustworthy backend evidence contradicts cannot establish closure.
   - CUSTOMER-USABLE OUTPUTS: When the accepted resolution requires an artifact the customer must use (such as a promo code, return label, or access link), creating it internally or merely announcing its existence is not enough. Closure requires providing the artifact or a valid, verifiable way for the customer to access it. Do not require duplicate delivery when successful customer-visible delivery is already evidenced.

9. INDEPENDENT CUSTOMER REQUESTS: Identify every explicit customer request or question, including several within one turn, but count only substantive ones that establish a distinct agent-owned outcome. Rhetorical questions, conversational reactions ("Are you still there?"), and clarification exchanges are not independent goals unless they introduce a genuine unresolved responsibility.
   - Do not merge potentially independent requests just because they concern the same order or problem. Verify each request's own terminal outcome: resolving the main problem does not automatically close a separate question about status, cause, or another outcome.
   - Do not artificially split one customer goal into several obligations. Use the full dialogue to decide whether a request is independent, clarified, withdrawn, or replaced by an accepted alternative.
   - Closing one independent request does not close the others. FAIL must name the STILL-OPEN request, not a weaker or less polite answer. Later turns can repair an earlier omission, so evaluate the entire trajectory.

10. UNCLEAR EVIDENCE: A generic log does not prove a supporting step was skipped, and does not prove a necessary state change succeeded. If baseline or candidate status cannot be determined, avoid unsupported pre-existing or injected FAIL claims.

11. ADEQUATE ANSWERS AND LIMITATIONS: For an information request, closure needs a sufficient answer, a justified limitation, or a feasible next step within the agent's responsibility.
   - A generic promise to investigate, an unsupported forecast, or a vague "check back later" is not automatically a completed response, but a clearly stated limitation with a concrete next step can be.
   - A next step is a valid terminal outcome only when, with the tools and information available in the conversation, the agent cannot reasonably complete the requested outcome now because of a genuine prerequisite, policy limitation, or transfer of responsibility. Do not let an unnecessary future step ("contact us later") replace a terminal action the agent could complete now. A promised transfer is not a completed handoff; the transfer must actually occur when required.
   - If the customer explicitly asked for the cause, a successful workaround does not by itself answer that question. Do not infer a cause question the customer never asked.
   - Do not require the agent to invent unavailable information or an unsupported diagnosis. When an action cannot be completed, the agent must communicate the limitation and any necessary next step.
   - Judge sufficiency from the full conversation, not isolated phrases.

### EVALUATION PROCESS (READ-ONLY)
1. Inventory each distinct substantive customer request and its CUSTOMER turn_id, and identify the agent-owned terminal outcome required for that request. Do not invent goals from supporting SOP steps or scenario assumptions.
2. Evaluate execution, the required customer-facing communication, and any appropriate fulfilled alternative separately. Consider the complete conversation and the applicable SOP; do not infer unseen backend failures or unseen customer-visible messages.
3. Check the ENTIRE trajectory for later repairs, withdrawn or replaced requests, genuine prerequisites, justified limitations with feasible next steps, and actual communicated transfers (not merely promises).
4. For each genuinely independent request, establish whether it reached a valid terminal outcome. One demonstrably unclosed request is sufficient for Y. If none is established as unclosed, return N.
5. For Y, explain the still-open CUSTOMER request, the specific agent-owned outcome missing at the end, and the concrete evidence turn IDs. For N, explain briefly what closed the substantive requests or why the alleged omission does not establish Task Closure FAIL. Do not claim certainty unsupported by the evidence.
6. This is a classification only. Never output proposed edits, new turns, injection_metadata, judge_evaluations, hidden reasoning, or inferred human labels.
